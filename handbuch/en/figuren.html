Map and scene

Figures and paths

Figures walk to their navgoals and ships fly their moves - you see the whole path and can change it on the map.

Selecting figures and ships#

Click a figure or a ship in the view to select its entity - the spawner of the figure, the brush entity of the ship. Marker and gizmo follow the model while it moves, and an outline shows what is selected. The arrow at the figure points where the model looks.

Start, end and new points#

  • Drag the end handle to move the walk target (the waypoint_navgoal) or the end of a flight.
  • Drag the start handle to move only the start, not the whole line.
  • Grab the figure in the middle of its walk (timeline between start and arrival) and drag: a new point is created at that moment - a new navgoal for figures, a split move for ships.
  • Rotate works the same way: in the middle of a flight the rings create a new point with the new angles. For a figure (NPC) they write set ( SET_DYAW ) at that time - the figure turns with its turn rate, as in the game; for the player it stays set ( SET_ANGLES ), which turns at once. While walking it applies when the figure arrives, because the game turns walking figures toward their direction. If a watch target is active at that moment, the status line warns: in the game the figure keeps facing it.

Walk or run, shared targets#

Right-click the end handle of a walk (or its band in the timeline) for its menu:

  • Walk (from this walk on) / Run (from this walk on) - writes SET_WALKING or SET_RUNNING before the navgoal.
  • Look at while walking - the figure keeps turning toward another figure while it walks (SET_WATCHTARGET).
  • Walk backwards (keeps looking back) - a look mark behind the start makes it walk backwards; Face the walking direction ends that.

When several figures share one navgoal, moving it would move all of them. BehavEd-Remake notices and gives this walk its own copy of the target first - the status line tells you which one it created.

Walking backwards is detected in existing scripts too: when a figure moves against the way it faces, the scene plays BOTH_WALKBACK1 or BOTH_RUNBACK1 like the game.

How figures move - like in the game#

The preview moves NPCs by the game's own rules, frame by frame: steering, movement physics and paths. What you see is what the game does with your script:

  • Speed. A walk uses the speed that is set when the figure starts - also when SET_WALKING, SET_RUNNING, SET_WALKSPEED or SET_RUNSPEED come right after SET_NAVGOAL. A new speed during the walk changes the rest of the way. Without a speed in the script the values of the .npc file apply.
  • Stopping. SET_NAVGOAL with NULL stops the figure where it is; a dowait on the walk continues. A new navgoal during a walk starts from where the figure is.
  • Turning. NPCs turn with their turn rate: 60 + 3 × SET_YAWSPEED degrees per second (330 with the usual yawSpeed 90). SET_DYAW, SET_VIEWTARGET and SET_WATCHTARGET turn smoothly, SET_ANGLES turns at once. rotate and move do nothing on an NPC - in the game too. Difficulty and game rules change the rate of some classes (Preferences).
  • Gravity. Figures stand on the floor of the map, fall, climb steps, stick to slopes and ride lifts - with the map's gravity and SET_GRAVITY. A figure that is spawned when the map loads is still falling when the cutscene starts, like in the game. DROPTOFLOOR changes nothing in single player.
  • Figures of the map. Every character that spawns when the map loads (an NPC_* entity without targetname) stands in the preview, also when no script names it - like the droid next to Anakin in md_am_sith. Classes such as NPC_Droid_R2D2, NPC_Kyle or NPC_Stormtrooper pick their character type themselves, as in the game; where the game chooses at random (NPC_Stormtrooper without spawnflags, the Movie Duels random spawners), the preview shows the first choice.
  • Paths. A figure walks straight to its target only where the game would: close by (under 110 units), between neighbouring waypoints (under 500) or with a free line (under 400). Otherwise it walks over the waypoint network, point by point - connect your targets with waypoints (NPC waypoints). Around corners a walk shows as several pieces of the same command.
  • No path. If the network has no path, the figure stands still and a dowait on the walk never ends - in the game too. The status list names the figure and the target.
  • Doors. Doors and other moving brushes stand where the game has them at that moment. An NPC waits at a closed door and walks on when it opens. A locked door (or one only the player can open) blocks the way while it is closed.
  • Ledges, curves and other NPCs. NPCs fall from ledges and land; curved surfaces (patches) block them like walls. They do not walk through each other: if two NPCs meet, the preview shows how the game lets them pass - and the times of dowait follow.
  • BS_NOCLIP. With SET_BEHAVIOR_STATE BS_NOCLIP a figure flies straight through walls to its goal, with the speed it has at that moment. Set SET_WALKSPEED or SET_RUNSPEED after BS_NOCLIP, otherwise a figure that has not walked yet does not move (and a dowait hangs).
  • OpenJK or Movie Duels. Under OpenJK rules walking NPCs re-steer every 100 ms (higher-ranked saber users from Jedi Knight on every 50 ms), under Movie Duels every 50 ms, and the turn rates per difficulty differ - so a walk can end a little differently. Choose the rules in Preferences.

Lightsabers: walls and clashes#

Lightsabers behave like in the game. A blade that reaches into a wall, the floor or a door is shortened to the surface and grows back; sparks fly and it sounds while it touches, and where it slides along the wall it burns a mark that glows briefly and fades after ten seconds. In water it boils. Sabers whose .sab file says noIdleEffect stay quiet while the figure only stands. Only figures the camera can see make sparks, marks and sounds - the game, too, only handles what is in the camera's view. Jumping on the timeline shows the same as playing through.

Blades of two figures clash only where the game lets them. Movie Duels: both NPCs must be in BS_CINEMATIC (set ( SET_BEHAVIOR_STATE, BS_CINEMATIC ), or a spawner with the CINEMATIC spawnflag), and the attacker needs set ( SET_SABERCOLLISIONS, true ); false switches it off again. OpenJK has no SET_SABERCOLLISIONS: there blades clash only in the animations BOTH_CIN_16 and BOTH_CIN_17 of figures in BS_CINEMATIC (Movie Duels allows these two as well). A hit plays the block spark, one of that game's clash sounds and the clash flare. Fights the AI would fight on its own (SET_ENEMY, BS_DEFAULT) are not simulated - only what the script animates. In Movie Duels the fast saber style also ignites the blade twice as fast. The rules are chosen in Preferences.
